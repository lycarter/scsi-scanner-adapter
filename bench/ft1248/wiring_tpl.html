FT1248 Bench Rig

Two changes to the 4-bit wiring, done one after the other. First, a 33 Ω resistor replaces each of the seven signal jumpers, as on the PCB. Then one data line moves to an ADC-capable pin.

Step 1: a 33 Ω resistor in every signal line

Unplug both USB cables. Pull each of the seven signal jumpers and put a 33 Ω resistor in the same two holes. The ground jumpers stay as wire.

33 Ω reads orange, orange, black, gold on a four-band part. The longest span is 12 rows, about 30 mm, which an uncut axial resistor reaches with its own leads. Keep the bare leads from touching where the four data lines cross; sleeve them or stagger the bodies.

If your breadboard rows differ from the drawing, go by pad and pin names, not row counts.

Then tell me, and I rerun the 4-bit tests at 30 and 37.5 MHz.

Step 2: MIOSIO0 onto an ADC-capable pin

After step 1 has been tested, move the Pico end of the MIOSIO0 line from GP16 (pin 21) to GP26 (pin 31). Pin 31 is on the same side as pin 21, ten pins nearer the Pico's USB end, right next to the RUN pin (pin 30). The breakout end stays on D0.

That is 21 rows, too far for a resistor's leads. Put the resistor from D0's row into a free row in the gap between the boards, and run a jumper from that row up to pin 31.

D1, D2 and D3 can stay where they are. This step runs in 1-bit mode, which only uses MIOSIO0, and the chip ignores the other three as long as they idle high.

Why step 2 is 1-bit, and a correction

On the PCB, MIOSIO2 and MIOSIO3 sit on GPIO 40 and 41, which are ADC-capable pads. Every pin used so far is an ordinary digital pad. The question is whether an ADC-capable pad can drive and sample a 30 MHz data line just as well.

I said earlier this meant moving one data line and rerunning the 4-bit test. That was wrong. A 4-bit PIO program needs its four data pins on consecutive GPIOs, and the Pico 2 W's only ADC-capable header pins are GP26, GP27 and GP28, with GP23–25 not brought out. No group of four consecutive pins reaches them.

The 1-bit test is the fair substitute. It puts the tightest path, a read sampled with the input synchroniser bypassed, through an ADC-capable pad at 30 MHz, and the same pad also drives the command and write data. It does not test four lines switching together on such pads; only an RP2350B board could.

Rejected: moving SCLK, SS_n and MISO to GP26–28 and keeping 4-bit. It would run in one wiring state, but it leaves the data sampling path, the one with the least timing margin, on ordinary pads.

Unchanged

  • Ground: Pico pin 23 and the breakout's Gnd next to D0, both to the ground rail.
  • "I2C Mode" switch off. Each board on its own USB cable. No power wire between them.
  • EEPROM: FT1248, clock idles low, LSB first, flow control on.
